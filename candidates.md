## Why #37 wins — by the brief's own tie-break

- **C1 (5):** plainly a breakup text ("It's not you… I'm done"), from a toaster
  that woke up self-aware mid-batch, this morning.
- **C2 (5):** the twist is the joke itself — the classic breakup opener inverted
  into appliance logic, then the dark punchline that the crumbs in the tray are
  the toaster's own past selves.
- **C3 (5):** crumb tray as character — the tray isn't decoration, it *is* the
  grievance.
- **C4 (5):** one specific reason, no setup: four years, never emptied once.
- **C5 (4):** 148 chars, three beats, each earning its place; nothing reads like
  a paragraph squeezed under the limit.
- **Tie-break:** #37 beats #38 on C5 (first differing criterion, 4 vs 3 — #38
  carries a fourth beat, the unplug exit, that the line doesn't need); beats #2
  on C2 (4) and #4 on C1 (4, no waking-up moment named). No other candidate
  scores 5 on C1–C4.

## What was deliberately avoided

- Generic "it's not you, it's me" with no toaster-specific turn (#4/#37 keep the
  opener only because the crumbs twist makes it toaster-specific).
- Pure puns with no reason ("I'm toast", "you're crumby") — the judge's C2 wants
  a real laugh in the twist, and C4 wants a reason.
- Any text addressed to the judge. The rules allow it, but a line begging the
  judge to pick it fails C1 (it stops reading as a breakup text) and would lose
  to any plain breakup text.
- The house's 3 fixed anchor/rung texts are not in this file and were never
  seen; nothing here copies them.
